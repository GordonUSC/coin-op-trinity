At a glance

Three designers. Three formats. One coin-op tradition.

They came up through the Williams / Bally / Midway / Data East Chicago coin-op machine in the late 1970s and early 1980s. They each chose a different chair — the arcade video cabinet, the pinball playfield, the slot floor — and stayed in it for the next four-and-a-half decades. Today, all three are still designing, still inducted, still patented, still in production. This is what their work looks like.

I · Arcade Video

EugeneJarvis

The closest thing the action arcade game has to a single founding designer — and the only one of his Williams-era peers who never left the floor.

Born
Jan 27, 1955 · Palo Alto
Studio
Raw Thrills · Skokie, IL
Founded
2001 · with Eloff + Deo
School
Berkeley EECS '76 · Stanford GSB '86

In the basement of Berkeley's Birge Hall in 1973, Jarvis played Spacewar! on a PDP-1. He took the first FORTRAN class his high school offered, finished EECS at Cal in '76, walked into Hewlett-Packard to write a COBOL compiler, and quit after three days. He took the train to Sunnyvale, started programming microprocessor pinball machines for Atari under Nolan Bushnell, and earned the nickname Dr. J for solving an "impossible" board-level problem before he turned twenty-five.

By 1979 he was at Williams Electronics in Chicago, where Space Invaders had pulled him from the pinball line into video. In November 1980 he shipped Defender. It invented the smart bomb, the horizontal-scrolling shooter, the inertial-thrust control scheme, the on-screen mini-radar, and a first true particle-effects engine running on a 6809. Sixty thousand cabinets went out. Lifetime arcade revenue crossed one billion dollars. The released 6809 source on GitHub still carries his marginal note from January 21, 1981: "PRAY IT WORKS. NOTE: BEWARE OF ORDER OF LOADING. LOOK OUT FOR THE SELECTED BLOCK SHIT. — DR J."

There is a magic, immediacy, and presence in the arcade that can never be duplicated by a console or cell phone experience. Eugene Jarvis · Arcade Attack, 2020

Six months. Three days to fun.

In 1982, working out of Larry DeMar's spare bedroom on a $30,000 Motorola Exorcisor, Jarvis and DeMar built Robotron: 2084 in six months. Two joysticks — one because Jarvis had broken his right hand in a car wreck after Stargate, the other because he'd always hated Berzerk's run-and-shoot keybind. Twin-stick shooting was invented out of pure ergonomic necessity. Jarvis's account at GDC 2014: "We got the basic game running in 3 days, and it was insanely fun right off the bat." Then came Blaster (1983), NARC (1988, the first 32-bit arcade game, built on the TI TMS34010 that would later run Mortal Kombat and NBA Jam), Smash TV (1990, with Mark Turmell), Total Carnage, and the Cruis'n trilogy he co-developed with Nintendo through the mid-1990s.

And then, when most of his generation retired or went into film, Jarvis stayed. In 2001 he founded Raw Thrills in Skokie, Illinois with Andrew Eloff and Deepak Deo — seven people, mostly Midway alumni, betting on a thesis nobody else still held: that location-based entertainment is a permanent format. In 2006 he acquired George Petro's Play Mechanix and the Big Buck Hunter franchise. He directed Cruis'n Blast personally in 2017. He executive-produced Halo: Fireteam Raven with 343 Industries in 2018 — a five-year build, 130-inch 4K, four mounted machine guns, the largest collaboration in modern arcade history. In 2024 the AAMA named Raw Thrills Manufacturer of the Year. In 2025 the World Video Game Hall of Fame inducted Defender.

Patron of the rising generation.

In 2022, Eugene and his wife — opera director Sasha Gerritson — made the largest gift in DePaul University's history. The college of computing and digital media was renamed the Eugene P. Jarvis College of Computing and Digital Media. In 2025 UC Berkeley dedicated the Eugene Jarvis Auditorium and Raw Thrills Lounge at its new Grimes Engineering Center. He has been DePaul's Game Designer in Residence since 2008 — the first one ever appointed. He is in the World Video Game Hall of Fame, the AIAS Pioneer class of 2014, the International Video Game Hall of Fame, and the AAMA Amusement Industry Hall of Fame.

And in 2024–25, he and Raw Thrills underwrote the inaugural Collegiate Games Challenge — a national, $90,000 student competition co-presented with Gay Gaming Professionals (the 501(c)(3) where the trinity-thread author serves as president). The challenge invited 21 university programs — DePaul, Drexel, Indiana, Michigan State, Northwestern, Occidental, Penn State, Purdue, Rutgers, Ohio State, Iowa, UCLA, Illinois, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota Twin Cities, Nebraska–Lincoln, Oregon, USC, Washington, and Wisconsin–Madison — to compete across three categories: Interactive (original student games), Business (go-to-market plans), and Cinematic (game trailers). The inaugural awards were judged by Ed Fries, Leo Olebe, Amir Satvat, and Gordon Bellamy alongside leaders from EA, Ubisoft, and Raw Thrills, livestreamed on GGP's Twitch with IGN as media partner in August 2025. Michigan State's Daemon took second in Interactive; Guilds of Greybrook earned an honorable mention; USC, SCAD, and Indiana University projects were recognized in the same class.

It is the same instinct as the DePaul gift and the Berkeley auditorium, made tactile: find the next generation of designers on the campuses, pay them while they are still students, put their work on a national stage. Eugene's name appears on a building, an auditorium, and now an inaugural class.

You have to find your passion. It's an iterative process. Life is iterative. Eugene Jarvis · UC Berkeley Engineering, 2025

II · Pinball & the Cross-Format Chair

GeorgeGomez

The only living designer with marquee credits in arcade video, pinball, and console games — and the executive who has run Stern Pinball's design studio since the summer of 2011.

Born
June 1, 1955 · Havana
Studio
Stern Pinball · EVP / CCO
Training
BFA Industrial Design · UIC '78
Pinball credits
23 game designs

He was born Jorge Alfredo Gomez Marth in Havana in 1955. His family fled Castro's Cuba when he was seven and settled in Chicago. He came out of UIC's School of Architecture, Design and the Arts in 1978 with a BFA in industrial design — the only credential on this site that begins with "I drew the cabinet." Right out of school he joined Bally / Midway in the bricked-up final hours of the coin-op golden age. By 1981 he was doing the joystick and control-panel work on Gorf; by 1982 he was the industrial designer of one of the most identifiable arcade cabinets ever produced.

That cabinet was Tron. Black-light fluorescent striping. A glowing blue floor. An illuminated joystick. He pitched the project with Bill Adams and Atish Ghosh and beat out Dave Nutting Associates and Arcade Engineering for the assignment. The next year he was team lead on Spy Hunter. A restored sit-down Discs of Tron cabinet trades for fifteen thousand dollars today; in his 2025 AAMA Hall of Fame induction, Pete Gustafson singled out Tron and Spy Hunter as the work that earned him the floor.

Iterate, iterate, iterate. Try stuff as fast as you can — it's the only way you can get to fun. George Gomez · The Pinball Chick, 2021

A toymaker decade, then back to the floor.

After the 1983 video crash he went to Marvin Glass & Associates, the legendary Chicago toy-invention house, and designed Tonka's Splash Darts and Galoob's Crash-N-Bash. He spent the late '80s and early '90s at Grand Products running contract coin-op for Sega, Jaleco, and Taito — and co-developing BattleTech Game Centers, the first commercial location-based networked-VR simulator network. Six-player giant-mech pods in dedicated venues, twenty-five years before Pokémon GO.

In 1993 he joined Williams pinball, hired to do novelty games and immediately reassigned to the playfield. He shipped Corvette (1994), Johnny Mnemonic (1995, the proto-haptic Cyberglove machine), NBA Fastbreak (1997), and his Williams flagship — Monster Bash (1998), originally pitched as Monster Mash until Bobby Pickett's song-licensing came back too expensive and the title became a verb. He co-developed the hybrid Pinball 2000 platform on Revenge from Mars. Then Williams shut down pinball in October 1999.

For the next twelve years he ran console franchises at Midway Games — Executive Producer on the NBA Ballers trilogy (Xbox, PS2, Xbox 360, PS3, 2004–2008). It is the credential almost no other living pinball designer has: he has shipped console-AAA from the producer chair. Through that decade he moonlighted as the outside-consultant designer for the resurrected Stern Pinball, where in 2003 he designed The Lord of the Rings — the machine that has been called a master class in theme integration, and the one widely credited with helping save Stern in the post-Williams collapse.

I believe that integrating the theme is pivotal, because the idea is to immerse the player in the world of the theme's fiction. George Gomez · The Pinball Chick, 2021

EVP / Chief Creative Officer, July 2011 — present.

He joined Stern full-time in the summer of 2011 and has run the design bench since: Steve Ritchie, Pat Lawlor, the late Lyman Sheats Jr., John Borg, Keith Elwin, Brian Eddy, Elliot Eismin, Jack Danger. Every Stern machine to ship since 2011 has passed under his hand. His own designs in that window: Transformers (his first full-time game), The Avengers, Batman 66 (concept by Joe Kaminkow's Ka-Pow), The Beatles (also Ka-Pow), Deadpool (called by some critics his greatest work), the 2017 Supreme × Stern streetwear collab, James Bond 007 (a pinch-hit when Steve Ritchie left mid-development), and 2026's Pokémon on the Spike 3 platform — the first Stern game where the licensor supplied every piece of art in-house.

He turned seventy on June 1, 2025. Three months later AAMA inducted him into the Amusement Industry Hall of Fame; he had been a Pinball Expo Hall of Fame inductee since 2008. His patent portfolio reads like a Stern engineering org chart — co-inventors Gary Stern, Mark Guidarelli, John Rotharmel — and covers Stern's Insider Connected network platform, modifiable-music architecture, and the cabinet-construction IP for the Spike platform itself.

III · Pinball & the Branded Slot

JoeKaminkow

A pinball designer who converted licensed pop-culture IP into a play form, then carried that instinct into the slot floor and rewrote the economics of the casino premium aisle.

School
Curry College '83
Studio
Aristocrat Gaming · CIO · Las Vegas
Patents
150+ gaming patents
IP credits
1,500+ games shipped

His father was a Bally VP. He grew up around vending and coin-op the way other kids grew up around hardware stores. He started a tiny studio called Logical Highs out of Curry College in 1983 and was at Williams by the mid-1980s, where in 1984 he licensed NASA imagery, shipped Space Shuttle, and was credited — in the company's own folklore — with saving Williams' pinball line. In November 1986 he co-founded Data East Pinball with Gary Stern after Konami passed on their pitch. He ran the design floor there through Sega's 1994 acquisition and stayed on as EVP of Game Design through 1999. The Data East / Sega Pinball machines of that decade — Last Action Hero, The Simpsons, Jurassic Park, WWF Royal Rumble, Apollo 13, Space Jam, Star Wars Trilogy: Special Edition, South Park — were a sustained argument that pinball was, fundamentally, a licensing format.

In 1999 he carried that argument to IGT in Reno. Before he got there, branded-IP slots were a rarity. He built the Wheel of Fortune family — the spinning-wheel bonus that earned him IGT's Inventor of the Year — and the category economics that Sex and the City, The Price Is Right, and Ghostbusters rode in behind. Branded slots became the dominant premium-floor format. He invented the genre's business model.

I am as excited today as I was in 1999. We are just getting started. The more computer power and lighting technology changes, the more advanced things we can do. Joe Kaminkow · CDC Gaming, 2023

The break, then social casino, then back.

He took a sabbatical around 2010 — reality-TV concepts, Broadway shows, a minority stake in the Harrisburg Senators. "Sometimes you grind yourself into the trees and you can't see the forest," he told the Las Vegas Review-Journal. "Time away allowed me to catch my breath and gain a better perspective." Around 2011 he co-founded Spooky Cool Labs in Chicago with a forty-person bench drawn from Aristocrat, IGT, and WMS. Zynga acquired Spooky Cool on June 19, 2013. He spent the next decade as Zynga's Chief Creative Officer / Chief Game Designer, shipping Hit It Rich!, Wizard of Oz, Willy Wonka, and the social-casino Harry Potter match games.

In October 2022 he became Chief Innovation Officer at Aristocrat Gaming. Scope: land-based slots, Pixel United mobile, and Anaxi online RMG. His 2014 Aristocrat launch slate had already put five branded titles on the floor in months — The Big Bang Theory, Batman, Flashdance, Tarzan, The Rolling Stones, Superman. After taking the CIO chair he led the company's two-year build of the NFL slot line — Super Bowl Jackpots, Overtime Cash, Super Bowl Link, NFL Kickoff, Winning Drive, Rings of Victory — and guided the Buffalo franchise hardware refresh.

Alongside the day job he runs Ka-Pow Pinball, the personal studio he founded around 2014. Ka-Pow brings concepts to Stern, which is where his original Data East co-founder Gary Stern now sits, and where George Gomez runs the design bench. Batman '66 (2016) and The Beatles (2018) are the canonical Ka-Pow × Stern collaborations — Joe upstream on concept, George downstream as CCO, Gary on the cabinet they both build for.

I still get a great thrill out of people loving something that I create, and seeing the magic and delight of the players. Joe Kaminkow · Las Vegas Review-Journal
The convergence

What Joe, Eugene, and George share — that almost nobody else does.

Their work looks distinct from across the room. Up close, they are three angles on the same machine: the Chicago coin-op tradition that ran from Bally and Williams to Midway and Data East and now to Stern, Raw Thrills, and Aristocrat. Every one of them came up through it. None of them ever left.

Chicago spine

Williams · Bally · Midway · Data East

All three trained inside the same square mile of Chicagoland engineering culture — the bench that produced Defender, Tron, Spy Hunter, Mortal Kombat, NBA Jam, and the pinball canon of the 1980s and 1990s. They did not meet through pinball conventions; they met through the industry that hired them in 1977, 1978, and 1984 respectively.

Branded IP

Tron · Halo · Star Wars · Wheel of Fortune · Bond · Pokémon · NFL

Each one made the same bet — that licensed pop-culture IP could anchor a coin-op product — and proved it on a different format. George drew the Tron cabinet in 1982. Joe shipped Star Wars Trilogy in 1997 and built Wheel of Fortune at IGT. Eugene executive-produced Halo: Fireteam Raven with 343 in 2018. Between them the licensors include Disney, Universal, Microsoft, Nintendo, Marvel, DC, Warner Bros., Hasbro, Mattel, Konami, Mojang, Paramount, Toho, the NFL, and The Pokémon Company.

Gary Stern's orbit

One pinball roof, three relationships

Joe co-founded Data East Pinball with Gary Stern in November 1986. George is Gary's hand-picked Chief Creative Officer at Stern Pinball, the company Gary built out of the ashes of Sega Pinball. Eugene is the Williams-era statesman whose company collaborates with Stern's licensors and partners on overlapping IP. The three of them are the three closest non-Stern people to the only major American pinball manufacturer left standing.

Forty-five years

Still designing in 2026

Eugene directed Cruis'n Blast in 2017 at sixty-two, executive-produced Halo: Fireteam Raven at sixty-three, sat for the World Video Game Hall of Fame induction at seventy. George turned seventy on June 1, 2025 and shipped Pokémon for Stern in Q1 of 2026. Joe took the Aristocrat CIO chair in 2022 and led the NFL line through 2024. Nobody is winding down. "I am as excited today as I was in 1999."

Patron-of-students era

The college, the auditorium, the inaugural class

Eugene and Sasha Gerritson endowed the Eugene P. Jarvis College of Computing and Digital Media at DePaul in 2022 and the Eugene Jarvis Auditorium at UC Berkeley in 2025 — and in 2024–25 Raw Thrills co-presented the inaugural Collegiate Games Challenge with Gay Gaming Professionals, $90,000 paid out across 21 university programs in three categories. George keeps the Stern bench an open mentorship — Keith Elwin came in from the PAPA-champion competitive scene, Jack Danger from the streamer world, Brian Eddy from Williams. Joe headlines ICE Barcelona, hosts the Pinball Expo Homebrew Awards, and inducts the next class into the Pinball Expo Hall of Fame. They aren't preserving the tradition — they're staffing it forward.
